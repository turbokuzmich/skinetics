# Cosmetic claims policy

Last reviewed: 2026-08-22

Skinetics product copy must describe cosmetics and cosmetic care, not medicines or guaranteed treatment outcomes.

## Acceptable content categories

- cosmetic purpose and suitable use context;
- verified ingredients and their cosmetic role;
- application instructions and frequency;
- precautions and compatibility information supported by labeling;
- realistic expectations using qualified language;
- full verified INCI or packaging composition;
- manufacturer, volume, and marketplace availability.

## Claims requiring evidence and review

- clinical, laboratory, or dermatologist-tested statements;
- quantified results or time-to-result claims;
- “proven effectiveness” or comparative superiority;
- safety claims for sensitive groups;
- claims connected to diagnosed conditions.

## Do not publish without explicit substantiation

- treatment, prevention, or cure claims;
- guaranteed hair growth, anti-hair-loss, anti-aging, or “botox-equivalent” outcomes;
- “completely safe”, “no side effects”, or other absolute safety language;
- claims that a cosmetic changes physiological structures or treats fungal or inflammatory disease;
- invented certifications, expert endorsements, studies, or ingredient concentrations.

## Review workflow

1. Compare proposed copy with packaging and manufacturer documents.
2. Mark every unsupported or ambiguous claim.
3. Rewrite into cosmetic, qualified language or remove it.
4. Record the reviewed source and date in the relevant phase findings.
5. Escalate uncertain legal or regulatory claims for professional review.

## Ownership

- Dmitry, business owner, approves ordinary product and brand copy.
- Medical, regulatory, quantified, clinical, guaranteed-effect, and otherwise uncertain claims require external professional or legal review before publication.
- Missing authoritative application or precaution wording must block product publication; do not invent fallback instructions.
